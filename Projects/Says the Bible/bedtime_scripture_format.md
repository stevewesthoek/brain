📄 BEDTIME_SCRIPTURE_FORMAT.md — v1.1

Purpose:
Define the standard structure, pacing, and production rules for all Saysthe.bible audio tracks.

This format ensures consistency, recognisability, and scalable production.

Variation occurs in story content, not structure.

⸻

0. Core Principle

Bedtime audio is environment, not performance.

The format prioritizes:

• Predictability
• Calm pacing
• Ritual repetition
• Loopability
• Low cognitive load

Every audio should feel familiar even when the story changes.

⸻

1. Standard Length

Core track length:

28–32 minutes narration

Rationale:

• Supports sleep onset window
• Affordable to produce
• Easy ElevenLabs generation
• Loopable into long videos
• Suitable as single product SKU

Long loops are separate products, not the base format.

⸻

2. Narrative vs Text‑Anchored Paraphrase vs Reflection Ratio

Target ratio (by time):

60% narrative (story flow and connective retelling)
25% text‑anchored paraphrase (close to the Biblical text, but not verbatim)
15% reflective calming application

Narrative provides flow.
Text‑anchored paraphrase provides fidelity to the source.
Reflection provides reinforcement and calm.

No verbatim Scripture.
No verse numbers or citations in the audio or script.

⸻

3. Master Segment Structure

All audio follows the same sequence.

Segment A — Gentle Arrival (1–2 min)

Purpose:
Lower arousal and signal bedtime.

Characteristics:

• Soft orientation
• No brand mention
• No explanation of product
• Simple present-tense language
• Slow pacing

This replaces a traditional intro.

⸻

Segment B — Story Units (Narrative + Reflection Loop)

Retold Biblical narrative using calm descriptive language, with reflection woven after each story unit.

Rules:

• Short sentences
• Concrete imagery
• Soft verbs
• Repetition allowed
• No emotional spikes
• Frequent paragraph pauses

Text‑anchored paraphrase is inserted naturally.

Reflection framework (required):
Observation → Meaning → Personal Connection → Permission to Rest.

⸻

Segment C — Anchor Block (Identity / Safety)

A slower, emotionally central section.

Purpose:

• Emphasize identity, safety, and care
• Slow the pace
• Prepare for the rest descent

⸻

Segment D — Rest Block (Reflection Heavy)

Rest‑focused reinforcement with low energy.

Typical motifs:

Rest is part of creation
You are allowed to rest
Nothing needs to be held together right now

Language must be gentle, simple, and repetitive.

⸻

Segment E — Loopable Closing

Purpose:

Allow seamless replay while the listener sleeps.

Characteristics:

• Slowing cadence
• Short lines
• Repetition
• Soft blessing tone
• No final “ending” language

Audio should fade naturally.

Use the identical closing pattern across all episodes.

⸻

4. Intro & Outro Policy

Avoid:

Podcast-style intros
Music logos
Brand speeches
Calls to action
Explanatory framing

The audio begins immediately inside the experience.

Brand lives outside the track.

⸻

5. Music & Sound Layer

Audio base includes:

• Soft pad or piano texture
• Pink or brown noise (low level)

Rules:

• No melodic hooks
• No musical changes
• No sound effects
• Consistent mix across tracks

Sound functions as atmosphere.

⸻

6. Script Formatting Rules (ElevenLabs)

Formatting is used to control pacing.

Guidelines:

• Short paragraphs
• Line breaks indicate pauses
• Ellipses used sparingly
• Bracketed stage directions allowed

Examples:

[slow pause]
[breath]
[long pause]

The script should read like breathing text.

⸻

7. Consistency Policy

Structure remains constant across all stories.

Allowed variation:

Story
Text‑anchored paraphrase choices
Length tier (future)
Language

Not allowed:

Segment order changes
Different intro style
Different pacing philosophy
Dramatic tonal shifts

Consistency builds recognition.

⸻

8. Product Alignment

Each formatted audio produces:

• One core track
• One product SKU

Base deliverables for each SKU:

• WAV master (24-bit / 48kHz)
• MP3 (320 kbps)

Loop or ambient variants are optional separate SKUs or YouTube-only assets.

The format ensures each story becomes inventory.

⸻

9. Visual Alignment

Each audio pairs with:

• One illustration
• Slow motion preset
• Matching thumbnail
• Printable asset reuse

Audio and visuals share the same stability principle.

⸻

10. Evolution Rules

The format evolves slowly.

Potential future additions:

Extended loops
Age-specific pacing
Seasonal variants
Multilingual refinements

Core structure remains unchanged.

13. Pause Notation System

Standard markers:

[short pause] → breath
[pause] → sentence spacing
[slow pause] → emotional softening
[long pause] → section transition

Rules:

Do not overuse
Pauses follow paragraph ends
Never cluster multiple pause tags

Pause consistency creates recognisable pacing.

⸻

14. Text Fidelity Rules (Paraphrase)

Prefer story movements that are:

Narrative
Reassuring
Rhythmic
Simple to describe
Familiar

Avoid:

Genealogies
Dense legal material
Rapid perspective shifts
High emotional intensity

Paraphrase rules:

- Stay within what the text implies.
- Do not add extra doctrine or speculative detail.
- Keep phrasing calm and child‑level.

⸻

15. Voice Parameter Presets (ElevenLabs Baseline)

Stability: high
Style exaggeration: low
Speed: slightly slower than default
Clarity: neutral
Emotion: restrained

Voice must feel predictable across episodes.

One narrator identity recommended.

⸻

16. Loop Generation Rules

Loop variants are optional and not part of the base paid download.

Core track → base

Loop version created by:

Extending Segment E
Duplicating ambient bed
Maintaining same ending cadence

Loop must not introduce new narrative content.

⸻

17. Multilingual Adaptation Notes

Structure identical across languages.

Adjust:

Sentence length
Cultural phrasing
Text tone
Pause density

Never change segment order.

⸻

18. Production Checklist (Per Audio)

Script mapped to segments
Script formatted for pauses
Text anchors selected (paraphrase plan)
Voice settings locked
Ambient bed prepared
Illustration generated
SKU named
Master stored

Checklist prevents drift.

⸻

19. Quality Signals

Successful track characteristics:

Listener can start anywhere
Listener can fall asleep mid-sentence
Listener can replay without noticing repetition
Parent trusts consistency

These signals matter more than literary quality.
