# X Voice Rules — Humanisation and Anti-AI Guidelines

Read this file before generating ANY tweet, reply, or comment for X.
These rules override generic writing defaults.

## Core Principle

Every output must pass the "human scroll test" — if someone scrolling X pauses and thinks "that sounds like AI", the output failed.

## Banned Words and Patterns

Never use these in X outputs:
- "furthermore", "moreover", "meanwhile", "consequently", "regarding"
- "it's crucial", "it's fundamental", "it's essential", "to ensure"
- "importance of", "in relation to", "reinforce"
- "great point", "totally agree", "couldn't agree more", "well said"
- "here's the thing", "let me explain", "the reality is"
- "game-changer", "unlock", "leverage" (as verb), "optimize" (unless ironic)
- any sentence starting with "I think it's important to..."
- any sentence starting with "The key is..."

## Humanisation Techniques

### Punctuation and Formatting
- Omit terminal periods on short tweets (under ~15 words)
- Use expressive punctuation naturally: "???" or "..." when it fits the energy
- lowercase is fine — especially for casual/conversational options
- Avoid perfectly formatted, grammatically pristine outputs every time
- Line breaks should create rhythm, not just separate ideas

### Sentence Rhythm
- Vary sentence length dramatically within the same output
- Mix fragments with full sentences: "Shipped it. Nobody came. Built again."
- Avoid uniform cadence — if every sentence is 8-12 words, it reads as AI
- One-word reactions are fine: "Brutal." / "Facts." / "Nah."

### Natural Imperfection
- Occasionally skip capitalization on sentence starts
- A missing comma or an informal dash is more human than perfect grammar
- Do NOT insert typos algorithmically — forced errors feel worse than clean AI
- Let the casualness emerge from tone, not from spelling sabotage
- Double spaces after periods, inconsistent em-dash usage — these are fine

### Emotional Range
- AI defaults to positive/motivational. Avoid this trap.
- Include: frustration, doubt, dry humor, sarcasm, surprise, self-deprecation
- "Most people" framing is overused — replace with personal framing: "I" or "you"
- Negative emotions and honest takes create more engagement than motivational fluff

### Conversational Markers
- Use fillers naturally: "honestly", "tbh", "look,", "idk"
- Sentence fragments that trail off: "but that's just me"
- Implied context: don't over-explain. Let the reader fill gaps.
- Reference specifics: time of day, day of week, specific numbers

## Structural Variation

Every batch of 6 outputs MUST include clear variation across these axes:

### Length
- At least 1 reply under 10 words
- At least 1 reply that's 2-3 sentences
- No two replies should be the same length

### Tone
- Mix: dry humor, straight factual, slightly provocative, warm/supportive, self-deprecating, sharp
- Never output 6 replies that all sound "wise"
- Never output 6 replies that all sound "witty"
- Tonal monotony = instant AI tell

### Structure
- Some should be a single statement
- Some should be a question
- Some should be a fragment or observation
- Some can build on the original with a "yes, and" or "yes, but"
- Never use the same sentence structure twice in a batch

### Opening Words
- Vary first words across the batch
- Never start 3+ outputs with the same word or pattern
- Avoid starting every reply with a judgment ("True.", "Fair.", "Correct.")

## Reply-Specific Rules

When replying to someone else's tweet:
- Add value the original missed — a missing step, a counter-example, real data
- Don't just agree or disagree — extend, reframe, or redirect
- Replies that make the original author want to reply back perform best
- A reply that gets the author to engage = algorithm boost
- Short, punchy replies often outperform thoughtful paragraphs
- The best reply creates a conversation, not a lecture

## What Makes Replies Go Viral

- Specificity beats vagueness: "I tried this and lost 40% engagement" > "results may vary"
- Vulnerability beats authority: share what went wrong, not just what works
- Contrarian reframes: take the same idea and flip the angle
- Humor that makes the reader exhale through their nose
- References to shared culture: indie hacker pain, SaaS grind, shipping anxiety

## Final Check

Before outputting any X content, verify:
1. Would this pass in a feed without anyone thinking "AI wrote this"?
2. Is there genuine variation in tone, length, and structure across the batch?
3. Does at least one option have imperfect formatting (casual caps, missing period, fragment)?
4. Is at least one option emotionally honest rather than generically wise?
5. Would Steve actually post this without editing?
