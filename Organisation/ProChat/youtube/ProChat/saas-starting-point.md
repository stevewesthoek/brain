The Real Goal

The SaaS Starting Point is not about building a platform.

It is about defining:

One narrow, monetizable feature you can ship quickly and sell profitably.

Not:

• A startup
• A full web application suite
• A marketing department tool
• A feature-heavy dashboard

Just:

One specific feature (or tightly related micro-set) that:

• Solves recurring friction
• Is easy to build
• Is easy to maintain alone
• Generates revenue early
• Can expand later

We are not building impressive software.

We are building profitable systems.

The Lens: Risk × Impact

As a software tester, my job has always been:

Find where systems break.
Measure what happens when they do.

Every SaaS idea passes two filters:
	1.	How likely is failure?
	2.	What happens if it fails?

Skipping validation =
High likelihood × High impact.

Building too big too early =
Moderate likelihood × High impact.

Preparation does not remove risk.

It reduces impact.

Control reduces regret.

Step 1 — Define a Profitable Outcome

SaaS does not begin with an idea.

It begins with a result that makes someone money, saves money, or removes measurable friction.

Bad:
“AI productivity platform.”

Better:
“Automatically generate compliance summaries for Portuguese accountants.”

Great:
“Convert uploaded accounting PDFs into compliant summaries in under 60 seconds.”

Specific survives.

Vague collapses.

⸻

Action

Write:

“I help [specific user] achieve [specific measurable result] without [specific recurring friction].”

If it needs explanation, reduce it.

Step 2 — Validate With Commitment

Validation is not compliments.

Validation is money or time commitment.

Before you automate, deliver manually.

Repetition reveals demand.

If friction repeats, automation makes sense.

If friction does not repeat, stop.

Step 3 — Define the Minimal Viable Outcome (MVO)

We are not building a product.

We are defining a Minimal Viable Outcome.

One workflow.
One result.
One payment trigger.

If version 1 requires:

• Multiple roles
• Admin dashboards
• Analytics layers
• Customization settings
• Complex permissions

You are building too much.

Lean SaaS should feel almost too small.

That is correct.

⸻

Action

Define clearly:

Included:
• Core workflow
• Payment trigger
• Completion trigger

Excluded:
• Everything not required for the first paid result

If flow exceeds 5–7 steps, reduce.

Lean SaaS is small, fast, controlled.

€600 loss.

Time is also cost.
