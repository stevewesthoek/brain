The Lens: Risk × Impact

For over a decade, my work as a software tester had one goal:

Find where systems break.
Measure what happens when they do.

Not in theory.
In production.

Every SaaS idea passes two filters:
	1.	How likely is failure?
	2.	What happens if it fails?

Skipping validation:
High likelihood.
High impact.

Choosing expensive infrastructure too early:
Moderate likelihood.
High financial impact.

Preparation does not remove risk.

It reduces impact.

Control reduces regret.

What You Actually Need to Start

You do not need:
	•	Venture capital
	•	A technical co-founder
	•	Advanced engineering knowledge

You need:
	•	5–10 focused hours per week
	•	Minimal financial exposure during preparation
	•	Emotional discipline
	•	A fixed toolset (no shiny object switching)

SaaS rewards consistency.
Not bursts of enthusiasm.

Step 1 — Define a Narrow Outcome

SaaS does not begin with an idea.

It begins with a result.

Bad:
“AI productivity platform.”

Better:
“Automatically generate compliance summaries for Portuguese accountants.”

Specific outcomes survive pressure.

Vague outcomes collapse.

⸻

Action

Write one sentence:

“I help [specific user] achieve [specific result] without [specific friction].”

If it requires multiple sentences,
it is not narrow enough.

⸻

AI Prompt

You are a product strategist and risk analyst.

Evaluate this SaaS concept:

Target user:
[INSERT]

Intended outcome:
[INSERT]

Your task:
	1.	Rewrite into a single narrow, testable sentence.
	2.	Identify scope vagueness.
	3.	Reduce scope by 50%.
	4.	Highlight structural risks.

Do not suggest new features.
Focus only on clarity.

Return:
• Revised outcome
• Risk analysis
• Simplified version

⸻

Step 2 — Validate With Commitment

Validation is not compliments.

Validation is commitment.

“Sounds cool” means nothing.

Commitment looks like:
	•	Payment
	•	Pre-order
	•	Signed agreement
	•	Allocated time

Before you automate,
deliver manually.

Repetition reveals real demand.

Repeated friction becomes automation opportunity.

⸻

Action

Deliver the outcome manually.

Track:
	•	What repeats
	•	Where friction occurs
	•	What clients struggle with

If friction repeats, it’s viable.

If it doesn’t, it’s noise.

Step 3 — Define the Minimal Viable Outcome (MVO)

We are not building a “product.”

We are defining a Minimal Viable Outcome.

One workflow.
One result.
One clear completion trigger.

If your first version needs:
	•	Roles
	•	Analytics dashboards
	•	Integrations
	•	Advanced settings

You are expanding too early.

⸻

Action

Clearly define:

Included:
• Core workflow
• Payment trigger
• Completion trigger

Excluded:
• Everything not required for the first paid result

If your flow exceeds 5–7 core steps,
reduce it.

Complexity compounds fragility.

⸻

	2.	Remove non-essential elements.

	4.	Identify single payment trigger.

Assume the founder is non-technical.
Keep complexity low.

Step 4 — Count the Cost

Most founders avoid this step.

Ask:
	•	Monthly infrastructure cost?
	•	Weekly time investment?
	•	Timeline to first revenue?
	•	Worst-case financial loss?

If you build for 6 months at $250/month:

$1,500 lost.

Now calculate time.

Risk × Impact.

When numbers are visible,
fear decreases.

Control increases.
